What you get

Everyone in the office works from the same list.

Whoever places the orders, whoever runs the job and whoever meets the truck can each see where a material stands without asking anyone.

Every material on one list

Each job's selections come in from a spreadsheet or an export, and every material keeps its status, dates, vendor, rep and model number. When the selections change, bring in the new export and it shows what is new, what changed and what may have been dropped.

Know what to order and when

The dashboard works back from the day a material is needed on site and the vendor's lead time, and shows what has to be ordered now. It also shows what is due this week and which orders have gone quiet.

One email every morning

Each morning the team gets one email with what is waiting for an OK, what needs ordering, what is due today, what was flagged and what arrived. On a day with nothing to report, it does not send.

The crew works the same list

Invite the people who order, receive or need to know, and everyone sees the same jobs. When a superintendent checks in a delivery on the field app, the office sees it as soon as it is saved.

Vendor mail keeps it current

Connect the inboxes your vendor mail already comes into, or forward from them, and Material Monitor suggests updates with the vendor's words quoted. Somebody in the office confirms each one before it goes on the list.

The field app

The crew carries the same list on site.

The field app is the crew's half of Material Monitor, for the superintendent or whoever meets the truck. What they record on site is in front of the office right away, and what the office changes shows up on their phones.

Receive a delivery.

Check off what came, mark anything short, damaged or wrong, note where it was put and add a photo.

Look up a material.

Search any job by room, material or model number and see where it stands, when it is due and who the rep is.

Report a problem.

Missing, wrong, damaged or needed for an inspection. A sentence goes to the office by email and stays on their board until it is handled.

The morning email

It reads the inboxes you already have.

Vendor mail comes into real people's inboxes, so you can connect or forward several and label each one: the coordinator's, the project manager's, the estimator's, an in-house designer's or a shared one. It only asks about a new sender when a message looks like an order, a shipment, a delivery or a quote.

Nothing changes without a confirm.

Everything it reads becomes a suggestion with the vendor's sentence quoted beside it, and a person confirms or dismisses it. Forwarded mail always waits for a person, and no email can mark a material received. The one exception is a shipping notice in a connected inbox whose tracking number is already on the material, which updates the tracking and the expected date by itself.

It never writes to your vendors.

Follow-ups are drafts that go out from your own email when you send them. It never sends, replies, moves or deletes anything in an inbox.

Why this exists

It started as one office's spreadsheet problem.

Lock Ten Studio is run by the co-owner of a custom home building company outside Washington DC. His office tracked finish materials the way most do, with a spreadsheet, a few people's inboxes and the coordinator's memory. Orders went quiet, dates slipped and the office found out when the plumber was standing in an empty bathroom.

So he built one list for his office and his crews that shows where every order stands and what needs attention each morning. Common questions

Things builders ask.

Do my vendors have to do anything?

No. They keep emailing the same people they email now. There is no portal for them, nothing to sign up for and no new address to learn.

Do I need Buildertrend?

No. Each job's list comes in from a spreadsheet, a PDF or a selections export from the software you already use, and anything missing you add in a line. It works beside Buildertrend if you have it, and it never logs in to any other system.

Can my crew use it on site?

Yes. The field app is the crew's half of the same list, on an iPhone: they receive deliveries, look up any material on any job and report problems to the office. The office invites them from the console, and they sign in with a six digit code sent to their email. The app is in Apple's review for the App Store now, and founding builders can put it on their crews' phones through TestFlight in the meantime.

Whose inbox does it read?

Only the ones your office connects or forwards from. Vendor mail usually comes into real people's inboxes, like the coordinator's, the project manager's, the estimator's or an in-house designer's. Some offices also have a shared one. You can connect several and give each one a name. It only reads. It never sends, replies, moves or deletes anything, and it only asks about a new sender when a message looks like an order, a shipment, a delivery or a quote.

Microsoft 365 or Google?

Microsoft 365 inboxes connect directly today. Forwarding to your own private Material Monitor address works from any email system, Gmail included, and connecting a Google inbox directly is being finished.

Does it email my vendors?

Never on its own. When an order needs a word, it drafts the follow-up, one email per rep with every open item on it, and you send it from your own email in your own words. Your reps hear from you.

What happens when it reads something wrong?

You dismiss it and nothing changes. Everything it reads is a suggestion with the vendor's own words quoted beside it, so a wrong reading is easy to spot, and you can correct a suggestion before you confirm it. The one thing that updates by itself is a shipping notice in a connected inbox whose tracking number is already on the material, and that can only change the tracking, the expected date and whether it has shipped.

How does an office get set up?

Somebody in the office signs in at app.materialmonitor.app with a work email and a six digit code, names the company, adds the jobs, brings in each job's selections from a spreadsheet or an export and invites the team. Connecting vendor mail is the last step, and the list works without it.
